Three ways in, one studio

The app. Your AI. Your terminal.

Fuchsia Studio Pro opens the same studio to three ways of working — and when they are pointed at the same store they are one project: the same tape, the same clips, the same running order, whichever way you come in. Assemble by hand in the morning, ask your assistant to find something in the afternoon, let a script run overnight — it's one piece of work you keep coming back to, not three copies that drift apart.

In the app

The full studio

The complete Fuchsia Studio, exactly as it ships — the whole craft, from a folder of tape to the finished audio.

From your own AI · MCP

Put your assistant to work

Connect Studio to an AI assistant app you already use — any assistant that can launch a local MCP server over stdio, the open standard for this — and put it to work on your tape: find the moments, propose the cuts, read back what a Reading already found. What Studio hands your assistant is quoted verbatim from your tape, or refused. What your assistant then writes around those quotes is its own, and no mechanism of ours polices it.

From the command line · CLI

Script it and batch it

Script the parts of the work that repeat, and batch them to run unattended. Finish the same way: export a finished, loudness-targeted file — WAV, AIFF, AAC, Apple Lossless or FLAC — with no app window open. Headless, repeatable, CI-friendly. If your tape came in at mixed sample rates, it renders transparently anyway.

Still honest. Still yours.

How Pro will be sold

Get in touch to discuss pricing and availability.

To find out more about Studio Pro availability and pricing, get in touch. Pro won't be sold through the Mac App Store, in order that we can provide features the App Store doesn’t support. Talk to us directly about how to obtain it from Fuchsia.

Why it’s not on the App Store

Studio Pro adds two ways to use Studio without a UI: a command line, and an MCP server your own AI assistant connects to. We’d love to sell it through the Mac App Store, but Apple’s safety rules mean it would have to run inside a sandbox — which means a terminal couldn’t reach it. That’s why we sell it directly.
